# Contextual creative review

Watch each complete film with sound before judging. Use source content and the approved brief as the reference, without the creator's self-assessment.

| Dimension | Review question |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | Is there a memorable organizing concept that serves the message? |
| Clarity | Can the intended audience understand and recall the claim? |
| Truthfulness | Do copy, UI and footage represent the supplied evidence accurately? |
| Visual craft | Are composition, typography and image choices purposeful and coherent? |
| Motion and sound | Do pacing, continuity, accents and silence support the idea? |
| Originality | Does the film make a meaningful choice beyond a default template? |
| Reliability | Is the delivered file correct, complete and editable as promised? |

Record concrete timestamps, the observed effect and the reason for each judgment. A restrained film can outperform an elaborate one. Do not reward particle count, mandatory music, a particular palette or similarity to a named director. Permit ties and tradeoffs. Report technical failures separately from creative preferences; include unresolved content errors prominently.

For the craft pilot, examine hierarchy and reading holds, truthful evidence, coherent light/material, meaningful mask boundaries, motivated spatial movement and actual sound relationships. Record observed benefits and costs at timestamps. These are questions, not a required effect checklist; a film need not use any particular family. A library-free or adapted approach is valid. Originality means content-specific choice, not novelty for its own sake. Report both variants' failures and allow ties; authored strong/weak labels are not reviewer conclusions.
