# Creative direction

Start with what the audience should understand, feel or do. Extract concrete nouns, meaningful actions, tensions, evidence and emotions from the source. Find a visual idea that belongs to this content. Preserve actual product capabilities and distinguish a concept illustration from proof.

Possible mechanisms include recursive scale, impossible space, semantic typography, causal chain reactions, parallel actions, world construction, material transformation, interface characters, sound-driven geometry and narrative reveals. These are optional starting points, not fixed plots or a menu the film must follow. Combine or invent methods when they serve the content.

Choose a coherent visual identity without assuming black-and-gold, maximal detail, 3D or high tempo is intrinsically better. Favor a memorable idea over many unrelated effects. Aesthetic references inform visual language; the user's topic remains the story.

Describe the intended movement of motion-dependent concepts in the brief. Still-frame review cannot prove a transition or simulation works. Test those mechanisms after approval, before committing to an expensive final render.

If important source information is missing, clarify it. Infer routine production details and state them in the brief. Do not block on every cosmetic choice.

When moving from an idea to execution, use the optional [craft router](craft-library.md). Start from objects, actions, changes and relationships in the actual content; form a visual idea before choosing mechanisms. The [selection guide](technique-selection.md) supports reuse, adaptation, combination and custom invention, including no catalog reference. Lighting, masks and spatial 3D can carry meaning; none implies a default dark style. The library is a source of knowledge, not a boundary on imagination.
