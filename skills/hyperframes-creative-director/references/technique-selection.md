# Content-driven selection

Use this as a reasoning aid. Skip already answered questions; do not ask the user to complete a technique questionnaire. Existing brief and keyframe approval boundaries still apply.

| Decision | Question | Useful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | What objects, actions, tensions, changes and relationships define this topic? | A specific visual opportunity |
| Communication | Must viewers understand a mechanism, believe a claim, compare outcomes or feel an emotion? | The scene's job |
| Evidence | Is the subject real UI, footage, data, an object or a concept? What belongs to the brand? | Truthful assets and constraints |
| Visual idea | Which transformation, material, motif, juxtaposition or spatial relationship expresses the meaning? | A concept before a technique name |
| Reference fit | Does a known mechanism help? | Reuse, adapt, combine, invent, or use no reference |
| Composition | What carries meaning, reveals it, connects it and emphasizes it? | Attention hierarchy; these need not be separate effects |
| Feasibility | Can it preserve legibility, source fidelity and the budget? | Implement or probe the uncertain mechanism |
| Observation | Does the actual frame, movement and sound express the idea? | Keep, repair or discard based on results |

## Open choice and composition

If an entry fits, reuse it without a novelty penalty. If only its mechanism fits, adapt its palette, scale, timing and medium. Combine techniques when they perform complementary jobs: light may reveal a product edge, a mask expose real evidence, and camera movement explain depth. Preserve a clear focal subject and readable result; do not make all three compete for attention. Complexity alone is not a reason to avoid light, masks or 3D.

When no entry fits, derive an expression from the content. Cross-domain analogy, physical behavior, a new mask, supplied footage or generated assets may be appropriate. For fragmented knowledge becoming useful, fragments might cast a coherent shadow before assembling. This is a hypothetical visual idea, not an observed demo. A free-text description is enough; no technique ID, taxonomy slot, library registration or additional approval is required. A restrained static composition can also be the best answer.

After direction approval, probe only meaningful uncertainty before expensive production: the shadow relationship, mask ordering, camera readability or sound arrival. If it fails, revise the mechanism while preserving the meaning. A concept sketch may precede approval but must be labeled as such. Full production follows the existing approval workflow.

Useful custom techniques may remain project-local. After actual inspection, note purpose, implementation, limits and evidence. Promote to shared references only when reusable value is apparent and repository editing is authorized. Do not automatically expand the library after every film.

## Brief examples

These are decision notes, not approved briefs or rendered evidence. Use the normal BRIEF technical fields and keyframe table in actual projects.

| Decision | Bright Chinese vertical product story | Custom concept with no catalog ID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Format | 1080×1920, 30 fps, 20 s; Chinese; mobile feed | 1920×1080, 30 fps, 15 s; English; silent |
| memory_point | “把分散的信息，变成清晰的下一步。” | Scattered knowledge can support a shared decision. |
| proof | Supplied capture of a real documented workflow; no invented speed multiplier. | Conceptual illustration, no quantitative performance claim. |
| visual_idea | Separate source excerpts align into a readable action list. | Fragment shadows form one coherent map before the objects assemble. |
| visual_language | Warm white, supplied brand blue, generous type, soft contact shadow; reflow into short semantic Chinese lines. | Daylit tabletop, paper forms, grounded shadows, deliberate holds. |
| selected_refs | Adapt editorial-hierarchy and evidence-reveal; mask only where it explains source-to-result. | No catalog ID. Custom shadow assembly expresses agreement; simplify to composited projection if geometry costs too much. |
| motion_risks | Small-screen reading, long headline, mask hiding evidence; probe the transition and final hold. | Shadow legibility during travel; probe one fragment and its projection. |

A dark hardware brief may benefit from rim light and spatial staging if the supplied product calls for it. A silent typography brief can use semantic layout and holds without music or a drop. A footage narrative can preserve its own material. Topic labels do not select recipes automatically.
